# Body Piercing Gauge Converter & Size Chart: user guide

This guide explains how to convert, measure, calibrate, and compare body piercing jewelry dimensions across gauge, millimeter, and fractional inch standards for professional piercers, apprentices, and studio clients.

## What it is for

The Body Piercing Gauge Converter translates dimensions across three measurement systems used in body art: wire gauge numbers, metric millimeters, and imperial inches. It resolves sizing discrepancies created by supplier variations, renders true-scale physical cross-sections on calibrated screens, and identifies mystery jewelry posts through digital caliper reverse lookups.

## Who it is for

This tool is designed for:
- Professional piercers selecting initial jewelry thicknesses or sizing replacement posts during downsize appointments.
- Piercing apprentices learning gauge equivalents, AWG origins, and initial placement conventions.
- Studio counter staff verifying caliper readings against packaging cards.
- Piercing clients checking whether jewelry matches their healed piercing channel before purchase.

## How to use it

### Converting between gauge, millimeters, and inches

1. Go to the `Size Converter` section at the top of the interface.
2. Choose a wire size from `Select Gauge / Stretched Size`. The tool fills the `Millimetres (mm)` and `Inches (in)` fields with corresponding values.
3. Alternatively, enter a dimension into either numeric field. Typing into `Millimetres (mm)` calculates inches, while typing into `Inches (in)` calculates millimeters.
4. Recognized wire gauges highlight automatically in the dropdown and table. Non-standard entries display as `Custom`.

### Calibrating true-scale screen display

1. Click `Calibrate Screen Scale` beside the visual reference heading.
2. Place a standard plastic card adhering to ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 (85.60 mm wide) flat against your screen.
3. Drag the slider until the guide box matches your card width.
4. Click `Save & Apply Calibration` to lock in your monitor pixel density and update the status from `96 DPI Fallback`.
5. To restore default display settings, click `Reset to Default (96 DPI)`.

### Inspecting the live visual circle reference

1. Observe the circular graphic inside `True-Scale Visual Reference` after selecting or typing any dimension.
2. Once calibrated, the inner circle shows the exact physical diameter of the selected wire.
3. Check the four summary cards: `Gauge:`, `Diameter:`, `Inches:`, and `Fractional:`.
4. If a large stretched size exceeds container boundaries, the preview scales down proportionally with an on-screen notice.

### Reverse caliper lookup for unlabelled pieces

1. Close your digital caliper jaws fully and zero the display.
2. Measure post thickness across the smooth wearable shaft. Avoid decorative ends, balls, flare edges, or threaded sections.
3. Take three gentle readings along the wearable area to confirm roundness.
4. Enter your reading into `Measured Caliper Thickness (mm):` under `Measure Your Own Piece (Reverse Lookup)`.
5. Click `Find Closest Gauge` to calculate the closest standard size, directional variance, and difference in millimeters.

### Consulting the honest reference table

1. Scroll to the `Honest Gauge & Size Reference Table` section.
2. Compare reference columns: `Gauge / Size`, `Derived AWG`, `Industry mm`, `Approx. Inches`, `Nearest Common Fraction`, and `Typical Starting Placements`.
3. Note rows with dual millimeter values (such as 10G, 2G, and 00G) reflecting differences between jewelry manufacturers.
4. Click any row in the table to load its measurements into the converter.

## Questions and answers

### What is the millimeter size of a 16 gauge piercing?

A standard 16 gauge (16G) body piercing piece has a nominal diameter of 1.2 mm (derived AWG value 1.291 mm, approximately 3/64 inch). It is widely used as the starting thickness for cartilage piercings including the helix, tragus, conch, daith, and rook, as well as eyebrow and nostril piercings.

### Why is 00G listed as both 9.5 mm and 10.0 mm?

Different body jewelry manufacturers follow different sizing standards for 00 gauge: American wire gauge derivations approximate 00G as 9.26 mm to 9.5 mm (3/8 inch), while metric manufacturers produce 00G as 10.0 mm. Because a 0.5 mm difference can cause tissue trauma in a healed piercing channel, the tool lists both dimensions and advises checking the packaging label.

### How do I measure my jewelry post with a digital caliper?

Close the caliper jaws completely and press the zero button to establish a clean baseline. Place the flat internal measuring surfaces around the middle of the wearable shaft, ensuring the piece sits perpendicular to the blades. Take three gentle readings along the shaft while avoiding decorative tops, threaded sections, and o-ring grooves.

### How does the screen card calibration work?

Different computer displays feature different pixel densities, meaning 100 pixels on one monitor can span a different physical distance than on another. By matching an on-screen guide box to a physical ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 card (standardized globally at 85.60 mm wide), the software calculates your screen exact pixels per millimeter ratio.

### Is a larger gauge number thicker or thinner?

In the gauge sizing system, a larger number indicates a thinner wire. For example, a 20 gauge wire is 0.8 mm thick, while a 14 gauge wire is 1.6 mm thick. Once sizes exceed 00 gauge, the industry shifts from gauge numbers to direct millimeter or fractional inch measurements.

### What is the difference between AWG and piercing gauge sizes?

American Wire Gauge (AWG) is an electrical engineering standard based on geometric wire drawing steps. The body piercing industry adopted AWG numbers as convenient conventions but rounded them to standard metric values (such as 1.2 mm for 16G and 1.6 mm for 14G) for clinical practicality and international manufacturing consistency.

### Which initial gauge size is used for cartilage piercings?

Cartilage piercings such as the helix, tragus, flat, conch, and rook are typically pierced at 16 gauge (1.2 mm) or 14 gauge (1.6 mm). Using these thicknesses rather than thinner 18G or 20G wires provides structural stability, reduces the risk of jewelry cutting through tissue under tension, and promotes healthy healing channels.

## Limits

This tool provides mathematical conversions and standard manufacturing references, but cannot assess individual human tissue. It cannot determine anatomical swelling allowance, tissue elasticity, blood supply, cartilage curvature, or client healing capacity. Sizing must always be decided by a qualified professional piercer who physically examines the client anatomy and considers procedural history, placement angle, and tissue dynamics.
